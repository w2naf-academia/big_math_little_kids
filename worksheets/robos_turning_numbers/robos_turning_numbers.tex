% ================= The story =================
\section{The story}

\textbf{\color{robo}Robo the Robot} lives on a number line. Robo stands on a number and faces
the way the numbers grow. Robo knows two tricks: \textbf{walking} and \textbf{turning}.

One day a mathematician asks Robo a very tricky riddle. To solve it, Robo will discover a
whole new kind of number, and one of the most famous equations in all of math!

\section{Trick 2: the quarter turn, $i$}

\begin{tryit}
\textbf{Try it!} Do a half turn using \textbf{two quarter turns}: turn left a little (a quarter
turn), then turn left again. You are facing backwards. It worked!
\end{tryit}

\begin{bigidea}
Mathematicians invented a new number called $\boldsymbol{i}$. Multiplying by $i$ is a
\textbf{quarter turn} to the left. It makes Robo walk off the number line onto a new
\textbf{up-down line}. Numbers on the up-down line are called \textbf{imaginary numbers}.

Two quarter turns make a half turn, so \quad $i \times i = -1$. \quad Riddle solved!
\end{bigidea}

\section{Robo walks around a circle}

\begin{tryit}
\textbf{Try it!} Wrap a string once around a cup. Then see how many times the cup's width fits
along the string.
\end{tryit}

\question About how many cup-widths long was the string? \blank[2cm]

\begin{bigidea}
It is always a little more than 3, for \emph{every} circle! That number is called
\textbf{pi}, written $\boldsymbol{\pi}$. It is about $3.14$.

If a circle is 2 steps wide, the path all the way around is $2\pi$ steps long, so
\textbf{halfway around is exactly $\pi$ steps}.
\end{bigidea}

Robo starts at $1$ and walks around a circle that is 2 steps wide. Fill in the boxes with where
Robo lands: $1$, $i$, $-1$, or $-i$.

\section{Euler's equation}

\begin{mathclub}{How a scientist writes ``walk around the circle''}
A mathematician named \textbf{Leonhard Euler} (say ``OY-ler'') found a way to write Robo's
circle walk. There is a special number called $\boldsymbol{e}$, about $2.72$. When you write
\[
  e^{\,i\,\times\,\text{steps}}
\]
it means: \textbf{start at 1 and walk that many steps around the circle.}
\end{mathclub}

\question Walk $\pi$ steps (halfway around). Where does Robo land?
\[
  e^{\,i\pi} = \text{\blank[2cm]}
\]

\question Now add 1 to both sides. $\;-1 + 1 = $ \blank[1.6cm]. So:
\[
  e^{\,i\pi} + 1 = \text{\blank[2cm]}
\]

{\small\color{gray}\textbf{For teachers:} multiplying by $i$ rotates the complex plane by
$90^\circ$ counterclockwise, which is why $i^2 = -1$ reads as two quarter turns making a half
turn. Euler's formula $e^{i\theta} = \cos\theta + i\sin\theta$ places $e^{i\theta}$ on the unit
circle at arc length $\theta$ from $1$ (counterclockwise), so $\theta = \pi$ lands on $-1$.
Robo's ``circle 2 steps wide'' is the unit circle (radius 1), whose circumference is $2\pi$.
Why $e$ in particular is the base that turns ``growth'' into ``rotation'' is a calculus fact
($\tfrac{d}{d\theta}e^{i\theta} = i\,e^{i\theta}$: the velocity is always a quarter turn from
the position) and is beyond this worksheet.}
